CONTENT

The bill would amend the Michigan Trust Fund Act to specify that, after September 30, 2026,
no money could be appropriated to or spent from the Community District Education Trust
Fund (CDTF) and any money remaining in the CDTF on or after October 1, 2026, would have
to be transferred to and deposited into the School Aid Fund (SAF). This action effectively
would close the CDTF.

Under the Act, the CDTF is used to offset the absence of local school operating revenue for
the Detroit Public Schools Community District (DPSCD) in calculating the State-funded portion
of the foundation allowance. If the CDTF does not cover the full amount, the General Fund
must reimburse the SAF for the remaining cost. The bill would remove a requirement that the
General Fund reimburse the SAF in that case.

BACKGROUND

The DPSCD was created by statute in 2016 to educate Detroit Public Schools (DPS) students.
Detroit Public Schools continued to levy 18 mills on non-homestead property, and the revenue
was used to pay off emergency debt taken on by the district rather than going toward the
operating costs of DPSCD. The statute specifies that the DPSCD may not levy the 18 operating
mills if DPS is levying those mills. This would have increased the costs to the SAF since the
local operating mills were not available to offset the cost of the foundation allowance for the
DPSCD. The costs for the foundation allowance are increased by the amount of local revenue
generated each year, as that money would otherwise be used toward the DPSCD foundation
allowance.

The CDTF was established in 2016 to pay for these additional foundation allowance costs. The
CDTF received $617.0 million in total tobacco settlement revenue and money in the CDTF was
used to offset the additional foundation allowance costs for the DPSCD. The statute that
created the CDTF specifies that if the costs to offset the foundation allowance are greater than
the money available in the CDTF, the General Fund must reimburse the SAF for those costs.

FISCAL IMPACT

The bill would have no fiscal impact on local government and would have no net fiscal impact
on the State, although there would be offsetting costs to different funds. The bill would
decrease General Fund costs by approximately $124.0 million in fiscal year (FY) 2025-26 and
increase costs to the SAF by the same amount. The FY 2025-26 School Aid budget
appropriated $124.0 million SAF to pay the additional foundation allowance costs for the
DPSCD. Absent the changes in the bill, the General Fund would still be required to reimburse
the SAF for those costs and the additional money appropriated would lapse into the SAF at
the end of the year. Since the bill would remove the requirement for the General Fund to
reimburse the SAF in this situation, it would reduce General Fund costs compared to current
law and increase SAF costs.
