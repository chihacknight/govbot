CONTENT

The bill would amend Public Act 261 of 1965, which governs the creation of county
parks and recreation commissions, to allow a county with fewer than 100,000
residents to reduce, from 10 members to seven members, the required number of
members on its county parks and recreation commission.

The Act allows a county board of commissioners to create a county parks and recreation
commission under the control of the county board of commissioners. The Act requires each
parks and recreation commission to consist of the chairperson of its county board of
commissioners, its county drain commissioner, its elected county executive or the chairperson
of its county planning commission, depending on the county's population total, and seven
members appointed by its county board of commissioners. Between one and three of the
seven members appointed by the county board of commissioners must be members of the
county board of commissioners.

Beginning January 1, 2027, if a county had a population of fewer than 100,000, the county
board of commissioners of that county could reduce, from seven to four, the number of
members appointed to the parks and recreation commission. The bill would maintain the
requirement that between one and three of these appointed members be members of the
county board of commissioners.

MCL 46.351                                                                         Legislative Analyst: Lauren J. Lahie

FISCAL IMPACT

This bill would have no fiscal impact on the State and no direct fiscal impact on local
government units. The bill would be permissive in nature and would allow counties with a
population of fewer than 100,000 to reduce the number of members on the county parks and
recreation commission. There are 63 counties that have populations under 100,000. If they
chose to reduce the number of members on the board it would save the county a small amount
of money through reduced compensation.
